1-2 students to develop a data‑driven strategy to predict, ahead of public announcements, which public companies are likely to participate in M&A—both as targets and acquirers—over a 3–6 month horizon, and to identify strategically compatible pairs. Outputs are company‑level probability scores to support event‑driven and merger‑arbitrage trading and market intelligence workflows.

Key questions addressed include whether public market data and corporate disclosures can predict M&A before announcements and whether unusual trading patterns contain early signals; the proposal also tests if Generative AI can extract strategic intent from filings, transcripts, and communications and learn consolidation patterns by industry.

Approach overview (can be tackled independently):

Component 1: Score and rank merger‑arbitrage ETFs/mutual funds by deal‑completion predictive power via ex‑ante closure probability modeling, calibration and outcome metrics (Brier score, log loss, hit rate, realized spread capture), and rebalance‑signal detection to anticipate spread moves around milestones; inputs include ETF/MF holdings, N‑PORT/N‑CEN, historical holdings time series, and merger‑arbitrage benchmarks, with standardized schema, deal/fund feature engineering, and back‑testing.

Component 2: Apply LLMs to extract quantitative indicators from unstructured text (e.g., “strategic alternatives,” “portfolio optimization,” “capital allocation flexibility”) in SEC filings, earnings transcripts, press releases, and financial news.

Component 3: Detect pre‑announcement market microstructure signals—unusual options activity, abnormal call buying, volatility skew shifts, price drift, large block trades, and sustained buying without news—as potential early indicators of M&A events. 

Data sources span corporate disclosures (10‑K, 10‑Q, 8‑K, transcripts, investor presentations, press releases), market data (prices/returns, volatility/liquidity, options), and external information (news, analyst reports, industry M&A databases).

Evaluation emphasizes ranking‑based metrics suited to rare events: precision among top predictions, hit rate within top‑ranked names, ROC‑AUC, and event capture rate; as an illustrative benchmark, identifying 10–15 future acquisitions among the top 50 predicted targets would indicate strong predictive power.

Expected impact includes earlier identification of high‑probability M&A candidates, signal‑driven monitoring of skilled fund rebalances around deal milestones, and explainable, structured insights to enhance both trading and market‑intelligence workflows ahead of announcements.
